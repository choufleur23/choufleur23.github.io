Work in progress · M.F.A. thesis, Duke University

On Displacement, Allotropy, and the Return of Waste

Abstract Tracing my family's demolished village in Suzhou, now the site of an e-waste recycling facility, this paper proposes three recursive stages of displacement: territorial, infrastructural, and ontological. Borrowing the chemical concept of allotropy, matter rearranged under pressure, it reads the village as a site where human loss and material persistence occupy the same coordinates.

Life Out of Balance, Exchange Out of Sight: Extraction in Godfrey Reggio's Qatsi Trilogy

Abstract Against readings that treat technology in the Qatsi trilogy as a shared milieu, this paper argues that extraction is a directional transfer from a source to a beneficiary, which the trilogy fails to identify. It contends that the films' aerial views, slow motion, and archival footage reproduce the extractive relations they critique, taking from resources, labor, and images while leaving its agents and beneficiaries unnamed.

Documenting Crypto Extraction: Two Parallel Documentaries on Bitcoin Mining in Texas

My undergraduate honors thesis at Cornell, awarded summa cum laude, examining how crypto extraction reshapes communities and landscapes through paired traditional and hybrid documentary production. Draws on anthropology, science and technology studies, and documentary theory to trace the entanglement of energy infrastructure with everyday life.
